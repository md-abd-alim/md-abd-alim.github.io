Competitor Analysis Strategy

Learn what competing practices do well and where the real gaps are

What Is Competitor Analysis Strategy?

Competitor analysis is the process of finding out who your practice actually competes with for patients online, and studying what they do better, what they do worse, and where they leave openings. For a dental practice, the real competitors are often not the offices you think of as rivals, but the ones that appear in Google Maps and search results for the treatments and neighborhoods you care about.

A useful analysis compares practices side by side on the things that influence local visibility: Google Business Profile completeness, review volume and recency, treatment page coverage, content depth, backlinks, and offers. The goal is not to copy anyone. It is to learn what the market expects and decide where your practice can reasonably stand out.

Done well, it turns a vague feeling of being outranked into a short list of specific gaps. It feeds directly into dental SEO consulting priorities, so effort goes where it is most likely to matter.

Why It Matters for a Dental Practice

Your real rivals may surprise you

The practice across town may not be the one taking your patients. Maps results and search results show who Google actually treats as your competition.

It shows what Google rewards locally

Comparing the practices that consistently appear in the top results reveals patterns in profiles, reviews, and pages that you can learn from.

It exposes content gaps

If competitors have detailed pages for implants, emergency care, or Invisalign and you do not, that gap is easy to see and often fixable.

It prevents wasted effort

Knowing which gaps are small and which are large helps you avoid chasing tactics that will not change your position.

It informs offers and positioning

Seeing how others present new patient offers, financing, and insurance acceptance helps you decide how to stand apart honestly.

It sets a realistic baseline

A clear picture of where you stand today makes later progress measurable instead of anecdotal.

Step-by-Step: What a Dental SEO Consultant Checks

Here is the order I follow when analyzing a practice's competitive landscape, from identifying who matters to deciding what to act on.

1. Identify true competitors

Search the practice's core treatments and neighborhoods as a patient would, from several locations, and record who appears in the Maps pack and organic results. Separate local rivals from directories and large chains.

2. Review the Maps pack

Note who holds the top three positions for key searches, how that changes by area, and what their listings have in common, such as categories, photos, services, and posting activity.

3. Compare Google Business Profiles

Check primary and secondary categories, service lists, descriptions, hours, photos, Q&A, and update activity for each competitor. Look for completeness rather than tricks.

4. Map treatment page coverage

List which treatments each competitor has dedicated pages for and how deep those pages go. Mark where the practice has no page, a thin page, or a stronger page.

5. Assess content and site quality

Review blog topics, provider bios, before-and-after policies, page speed, and mobile experience. Note which content actually targets patient questions versus filler.

6. Evaluate reviews

Compare review count, average rating, recency, and how owners respond. Look at what patients praise and complain about, since those themes shape reputation and positioning.

7. Check backlinks and mentions

Look at the referring sites behind each competitor: local organizations, schools, sponsorships, press, and directories. Identify links the practice could realistically earn, not buy.

8. Review offers and positioning

Record new patient offers, membership plans, financing, insurance language, and calls to action. Decide what to match, what to improve, and what to deliberately avoid.

What Happens If You Skip It

Without a structured look at the market, practices tend to guess, and guesses are often wrong in predictable ways.

Targeting the wrong rivals

Effort goes toward competitors who are not actually appearing in front of your patients, while the practices that are winning local searches go unnoticed.

Copying surface tactics

Imitating a competitor's visible features without understanding why they work can waste budget and sometimes backfire on quality or accuracy.

Missing obvious content gaps

A competitor may own a high-value treatment search simply because they built the page first. Without comparison, nobody notices.

Overreacting to ranking changes

Without a baseline, a drop or rise in position is hard to interpret, and decisions get driven by anxiety rather than evidence.

Matching bad offers

Racing to copy deep discounts can attract price shoppers and hurt margins, when a clearer message about quality or access might serve better.

Ignoring review patterns

If competitors are praised for short waits or clear explanations and you never learn this, you miss what patients value in your market.

My Approach

I treat competitor analysis as research that supports decisions, not a report that sits in a folder.

  1. Start with how patients search

    I run real searches for the practice's treatments and areas first, so the competitor list reflects what patients see.

  2. Compare like with like

    I compare practices of similar size and market, and note where a larger group or chain is simply not a fair benchmark.

  3. Separate gaps from strengths

    I record where the practice is already ahead as carefully as where it trails, so you protect what is working.

  4. Focus on what is actionable

    Each finding is tied to a specific change, such as a page to build or a profile field to fix, with a rough priority.

  5. Keep it honest

    I recommend learning from competitors, never copying their text, buying links, or imitating reviews.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know who my real competitors are?

Search your main treatments and neighborhoods the way a patient would, from different spots in your service area. The practices that repeatedly appear in the Maps pack and top results are your real competitors, whether or not they feel like rivals.

Should I copy what the top-ranking practice is doing?

Not directly. Learn what they cover and why it may help, then build your own version using your own providers, photos, and wording. Copying text can create duplicate content problems and does nothing to show what makes your practice different.

How often should competitor analysis be repeated?

A full review once or twice a year is usually enough, with lighter checks each quarter on the Maps pack, reviews, and new pages. Markets shift slowly, but a new practice opening nearby can change things quickly.

Can a small practice compete with larger groups?

Often, yes, for local searches. Smaller practices can win with complete profiles, strong reviews, clear treatment pages, and real provider information. It depends on the market, and some searches will stay hard to win.

Does competitor analysis include buying links or reviews like they do?

No. If a competitor appears to use questionable tactics, I note it but do not recommend matching it. Those methods can violate Google's policies and put your listing at risk. Sustainable gains come from earned links and genuine reviews.
