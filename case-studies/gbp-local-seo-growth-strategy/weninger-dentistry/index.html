Weninger Dentistry, PLLC

GBP & Local SEO Growth Strategy · Tampa, FL

Business Overview

Weninger Dentistry, PLLC is a husband-and-wife general and cosmetic practice at 4030 North MacDill Avenue in Tampa, Florida, with 26 service pages, a large content library and a supplied rating of 5.0 from 574 Google reviews. Patients searching the practice name find it first, but generic Tampa dentist searches sit around positions 14 to 22.

This independent portfolio study is a 52-page Google Business Profile and local SEO growth strategy, built from a supplied intake, a live review of the website, its sitemaps and seven competitor sites on October 19, 2026, plus third-party estimates. It is not a client engagement. It audits the profile and site, investigates a 16% keyword decline, maps cannibalization, and plans pages, reviews, citations, AI search and a 90-day roadmap. Every target is a planning objective, set only after first-party baselines replace the third-party estimates used in the study.

These are independent portfolio studies based on publicly available information and third-party SEO estimates. The businesses were not contacted, are not clients and have not endorsed this work. No traffic, ranking or patient results are claimed.

  • Estimated organic visits: About 1,000 per month, down 0.9%
  • Authority and links: Authority Score 19; about 1,100 referring domains
  • AI Search Visibility: Score 35, with 19 mentions and 21 cited pages

Current Situation

The audit shows a trusted, well-reviewed practice whose large website spreads its strength across overlapping pages instead of concentrating it.

Brand strong, local terms weak

The practice name ranks first across four variants, but dentist tampa fl sits at 14, dentist tampa and tampa dentist at 18, and tampa dentists at 22. One homepage serves all four overlapping variants.

Keyword decline of 16%

Keyword coverage fell 16% while estimated traffic moved only 0.9%, suggesting mostly low-volume losses. Causes such as the January 2026 site update or internal cannibalization remain unverified hypotheses until lost keywords and Search Console data are reviewed.

Thin or duplicated pages

Implant and veneer pages read as short against high-value intent. Two sampled area pages carry about 200 words of generic copy among 19 area pages, and the site holds 269 news articles, including three snap-on denture articles.

Homepage trust gaps

A live fetch showed no opening hours, map, doctor names or visible schema on the homepage. Three offers were listed as expiring October 31, 2026, so expired specials could soon appear on live pages.

Profile details unverified

The live profile was not accessible. Hours, categories, photos and posts could not be confirmed, and the profile lists retail items and brand names that need checking. The site shows 4.97 and over 500 reviews versus the supplied 574.

Differentiators not yet used

Dedicated IV and oral sedation pages, snap-in denture services and a husband-and-wife team stand out, since two of five reviewed competitors do not mention sedation. The site does not yet build its strongest pages around them.

What Happens If These Issues Are Not Fixed

If the structure stays as it is, the practice's strengths are likely to remain hidden behind brand searches.

Competing pages split signals

Nine implant URLs, five denture URLs and near-duplicate articles compete with each other, so Google may rank a weaker page, or none, for valuable treatment searches.

Lost valuable rankings go unnoticed

Without exporting and classifying the lost keywords, commercial terms may be dropping behind low-value noise, and nobody would know which URL used to rank.

Area pages may look like doorway pages

Nineteen pages built from one template with little unique local detail risk diluting focus and may be treated as low-value, while distant towns do little for Maps visibility.

Expired offers damage trust

Time-limited specials left live after October 31, 2026 present stale pricing to patients and weaken the credibility that the review profile builds.

Inconsistent trust data

Two review figures, missing hours and no visible doctor panel make it harder for patients and search engines to confirm one consistent, credible practice entity.

Unverified categories or services

Adding specialist categories or brand services the practice does not hold or provide could violate Google rules and mislead patients, so every entry needs verification first.

Recommendations & Action Plan

I recommend verifying and measuring first, strengthening existing pages before adding anything new, and treating authority and AI work as the final layer.

1. Secure access and baselines

Obtain Search Console, Analytics and profile access, then verify the profile, run a full crawl, capture Core Web Vitals and set a grid baseline. Third-party estimates are replaced with first-party data.

2. Investigate the keyword loss

Export lost keywords, classify them as branded, commercial, informational or irrelevant, map each to its URL, and recover valuable commercial losses on existing pages. Exclude the 1,900-volume hygienist-school term from every target.

3. Sharpen the profile

Keep Dentist as primary, add Cosmetic dentist and Dental implants provider only if supported, and avoid periodontist or orthodontist categories. Mirror the 26 website services, remove retail items, verify Invisalign, and use one booking link.

4. Fix homepage trust

Add hours, directions, a map, a doctor panel with credentials and one verified review figure with its source. Renew or retire the three expiring specials and confirm tracking events for calls, texts and forms.

5. Rewrite Tampa and priority pages

Make the homepage the clear target for core Tampa terms, then strengthen implants, snap-in dentures, veneers, sedation and aligner pages with one URL per cluster, FAQs and case proof, keeping stale duplicates out of the way.

6. Consolidate before publishing

Merge the three snap-on denture articles, two Invisalign versus braces articles and overlapping crown pieces, with redirects to the nearest useful page. After that, publish one or two quality articles a month linked to a service page.

7. Tampa-first location plan

Strengthen the main Tampa page and cover West Tampa, Drew Park and Egypt Lake-Leto as sections before building pages. Improve or consolidate other areas using per-URL impressions, clicks and drive-time evidence.

8. Add reviews, citations, schema and AI checks

Run an incentive-free review workflow with privacy-safe replies, verify listings without mass submissions, add Dentist and doctor markup, review links manually without a blind disavow, and log a repeatable AI query set monthly.

Roadmap

Month 1: Verify and protect

Provide data access, verify the profile and hours, renew or retire expiring offers, investigate the keyword decline, fix homepage trust items, run the crawl, and launch the review workflow with replies to open reviews.

Month 2: Strengthen pages

Rewrite the Tampa page and priority treatment pages, merge duplicate denture, Invisalign and crowns-versus-veneers content, and review area pages against Search Console data before changing any of them.

Month 3: Consolidate and extend

Review the backlink profile, complete citation cleanup, add schema, consolidate or redirect weak area pages, run the AI query set, re-run the grid and compare KPIs with baseline.

Expected Outcomes

The report states these as planning objectives, with numeric targets set with management only after Month 1 baselines exist.

Clearer paths to appointments

Calls and requests improve through hours, map, doctor panel and booking paths on the pages patients compare.

Stronger local relevance

Targets include page one organic and top-three map presence for dentist Tampa, top ten for cosmetic dentist and implants, and top five for snap-in dentures, sedation and Invisalign.

Recovered valuable keywords

Valuable lost commercial keywords are restored on existing pages before any new content is published.

One consistent entity

Site, profile and listings agree on name, phone, hours and review figures.

AI baseline and monitoring

Log AI mentions monthly against the supplied score of 35, tracking trends without any promise of citations.

More From the Report

Competitor set and gaps

Five direct candidates and an implant-focused specialist practice were reviewed from public sites, including offices on Gunn Hwy and Dale Mabry. Competitors list early hours, membership clubs, med-spa services and same-day crowns. Distances, ratings and categories were not verified, and competitor review claims are not used.

E-E-A-T and medical trust

The audit lists Dr. Scott Weninger, Dr. Michelle Weninger and Dr. Jeffrey Abril, with their degrees. Missing signals include licence details, memberships, years in practice and doctor-reviewed content. I recommend verified credentials, real patient stories and clinical content reviewed by a licensed dentist.

AI search readiness

Supplied platform indicators show ChatGPT, AI Mode and Gemini mentions but none in AI Overviews. The plan calls for direct answers on service pages, consistent doctor information, Dentist and Physician markup, and FAQPage markup only where FAQs are visible. These are indicators, not guarantees.

Important Notes

Search Console, Analytics and the live Google Business Profile were not accessible, so traffic, keyword and AI figures are third-party estimates and several items are marked Unable to Verify. Competitor ratings and categories were not confirmed, and area-page decisions are provisional.

Rankings, traffic and patient numbers cannot be guaranteed. Targets in these reports are planning goals, and the first month sets the baseline. Real return should be judged against your own data, such as the lifetime value of a new patient.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Weninger Dentistry rank well for its name but not for Tampa searches?

Brand searches return the practice first, but generic terms like dentist tampa fl rank 14 to 22. One homepage serves several overlapping variants, and the site lacks visible hours, map and doctor panels, so local terms are largely untapped.

What explains the 16% drop in keywords?

The report does not claim a cause. Because traffic fell only 0.9%, the loss is likely low-volume terms, but the January 2026 update and internal cannibalization are hypotheses to test with the lost-keyword export and Search Console.

Does the report recommend building more location pages?

No, a Tampa-first approach comes first. The main Tampa page is rewritten, nearby neighborhoods become sections, and the other area pages are improved or consolidated only after impressions, clicks and rankings per URL are reviewed.

Which review figure does the report use?

It uses the supplied 5.0 from 574 reviews, flagged for live verification, because the website states 4.97 and over 500. I recommend one verified figure with a stated source across the site and profile.

Does the report promise rankings or AI mentions?

No. Targets follow Month 1 baselines, AI figures are third-party indicators, and no traffic, call or revenue results are promised. It is an independent portfolio study using public information, not a client engagement.
