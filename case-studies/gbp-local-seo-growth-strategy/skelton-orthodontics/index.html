Skelton Orthodontics

GBP & Local SEO Growth Strategy · Fort Payne, AL

Business Overview

Skelton Orthodontics is an orthodontic practice in Fort Payne, Alabama, led by Dr. Aimee Skelton, a Fort Payne native with 25 years in practice. It treats patients from age 4 through adulthood and, in the directories I reviewed, is the only orthodontic specialist office listed in town. It holds a 5.0 Google rating from 126 reviews.

This deliverable is a 23-section Google Business Profile and Local SEO Growth Strategy dated October 2026, prepared as an independent portfolio study rather than a client engagement. It combines the baseline figures with public-source research on the practice's website, AAO and ADA directories and competitor sites. It covers a technical audit, a profile audit, Maps ranking, competitors, keywords, a service, town and education silo plan, E-E-A-T, reviews, citations, links, AI search, conversion, KPIs and a 90-day roadmap.

These are independent portfolio studies based on publicly available information and third-party SEO estimates. The businesses were not contacted, are not clients and have not endorsed this work. No traffic, ranking or patient results are claimed.

  • Planned build: A 17-page service silo plus 7 location pages

Current Situation

Trust is not the problem here. The website and profile are not yet organized to turn that trust into search visibility in the surrounding towns.

A thin organic footprint

The site earns 187 organic visits across 85 keywords, about 2.2 visits per keyword. Ranking terms are mostly brand names plus a low-volume long tail, and few town or service entries exist to grow from.

Ten services on one URL

Invisalign, braces, early treatment and the rest live as anchors on a single /services/ page. No page can target a term like Invisalign Fort Payne, and the Google profile has no individual service pages to link to.

No town pages

Navigation and the home page refer only to Fort Payne. Nothing targets Rainsville, Collinsville or Scottsboro, while Carter Orthodontics already runs dedicated pages for its Rainsville and Scottsboro offices.

Profile strong on reputation, light on relevance

Rating and reviews score A+, but services are not itemized, posts are infrequent, and secondary categories were not verified. The report grades categories B minus and both services and posts C.

Fixable site details

On the live site I found a Text Us button using an sms link that points to an email address, generic metadata, a typo reading self-litigating for self-ligating, and an early-evaluation link that goes to the About page.

Mixed entity signals

Directories show name variants, two doctor bio pages exist, and a separate dental practice sits at 2101 Grand Ave NW next door. AI visibility of 28 suggests assistants know the practice but have little structured proof to quote.

What Happens If These Issues Are Not Fixed

Leaving these gaps open could let a nearby rival and weak signals erode an otherwise strong local position.

Rival owns the edge towns

Carter Orthodontics already has a Rainsville office, town-level pages and a free-consultation hook. Without town pages of its own, Skelton may be passed over by parents searching for an orthodontist near Rainsville or Collinsville.

Service searches go unanswered

With every treatment on one URL, queries like Invisalign Fort Payne have no dedicated page to match. The highest-value leads may go to competitors whose sites give each service its own page.

Lost mobile conversions

A Text Us button that points to an email address can fail for exactly the visitors most ready to act on a phone. Each broken tap is a possible missed consultation request.

Reviews that do not work as hard

Without service and town detail in reviews, and with unitemized services on the profile, the 5.0 rating supports trust but contributes less to relevance for specific searches.

Mis-attributed listings

Name variants and a neighboring practice at 2101 Grand Ave NW create a risk of merged listings or reviews assigned to the wrong business, which would weaken both trust and local signals.

Thin AI presence

With two bios, merged services and no confirmed schema, AI assistants may keep naming other practices for orthodontist near me questions, and the practice may be named less often than its reputation deserves.

Recommendations & Action Plan

I recommend building structure first, then local reach, then authority, starting with fixes that need no new links. The order follows the report's dependency plan.

1. Capture baselines and fix quick wins

In week one I would run a grid scan, set up Search Console and GA4 events, and pull competitor profiles. At the same time, correct the Text Us link, the self-ligating typo and the thin metadata so the foundation is clean before anything is built.

2. Turn the profile into a service menu

Keep Orthodontist as primary and add secondary categories only after a competitor audit confirms they apply. List each service with a short description, use a roughly 520-character description, add 8 to 10 photos per month, and post weekly.

3. Split services into a silo

Rebuild /services/ as a hub with four parents and twelve children, 17 pages in all. Build Invisalign first, then braces, teen, adult, early treatment and LightForce, each with its own title, H1, FAQ and call to action.

4. Publish location pages in tiers

Tier 1 covers Fort Payne, Rainsville, Collinsville and DeKalb County. Scottsboro, Boaz and Albertville follow once proof exists. Each page needs patients by ZIP, drive time and local reviews, never copy-paste town swaps.

5. Run a review system

Ask at the smile reveal with a QR code and a text, using service-specific wording, and aim for 8 to 12 new reviews a month. Never offer gifts or screen requests by expected rating. Reply to reviews within two business days.

6. Clean up citations and entity

Unify one name and format across AAO, ADA, Sharecare and New Patients. Verify the Align Find a Doctor, Bing, Apple and chamber listings, and consolidate the two doctor bios into one page with Person schema.

7. Earn relevant local links

Audit the 438 referring domains first. Then pursue school and booster pages, the Optimist Club, chambers, referring dentists, pediatricians and local media rather than adding volume for its own sake.

8. Add the AI and conversion layer

Add Dentist, Person, FAQPage and BreadcrumbList schema and answer-first FAQs of 40 to 60 words. On the site, add a sticky call bar, a short appointment form, the 5.0 and 126 badge beside calls to action, and GA4 tracking.

Roadmap

Month 1: Foundation

Baselines and tracking, site fixes, GBP services and description, the appointment link, review system launch, NAP clean-up including the 2101 Grand Ave listing, and consolidating the two doctor bio pages.

Month 2: Growth

Publish the Invisalign, braces, teen, adult and early treatment pages, LightForce and technology pages, Tier 1 town pages, Tier 2 pages if proof exists, and four guides including Invisalign vs braces.

Month 3: Authority

Citations on Align, Bing, Apple, health directories and chambers, local links from schools and the Optimist Club, the schema and entity layer, answer-first FAQs, and a day-90 grid rescan and KPI review.

Expected Outcomes

These are the planning targets in the report, to be managed toward and re-checked monthly rather than promised.

Review growth

The plan moves reviews from 126 to 155 or more by day 90 while holding the 5.0 rating.

Organic reach

Targets are organic traffic from 187 to 250 or more per month and ranking keywords from 85 to 150.

Local pack position

The report sets top 3 goals for terms such as Orthodontist Fort Payne AL and Invisalign Fort Payne AL, and top 5 for the edge towns, measured against a week-one baseline.

AI visibility

The plan aims to lift AI visibility from 28 to 40 or more and AI mentions from 10 toward 18, tracked with Semrush AI tools.

Measurable leads

Consultation requests from forms and calls are tracked from day one, with planned increases of 10 percent by month two and 20 percent by month three over the baseline.

More From the Report

E-E-A-T for Dr. Skelton

Experience, expertise and trust are rated strong: 25 years in practice, a UAB DMD, a 33-month orthodontic residency, and AAO, ADA and state memberships. Authoritativeness is still developing. I recommend one canonical bio with Person schema, a clinically reviewed by line, a consented case gallery and local press.

AI search layer

The plan names ChatGPT Search, Google AI Overviews, Gemini and voice assistants. It calls for one canonical business name, sameAs links, plain-language service definitions, comparison pages and a quarterly check against the baseline of 28. It also notes that self-collected reviews should not be marked up.

Conversion fixes

Most conversion fixes are small. The plan includes service-specific button text, a working text link, a short appointment form with a clear confirmation, HSA and payment answers beside calls to action, UTM-tagged GBP links and GA4 events for calls, form submits and directions.

What I Would Measure

  • Google reviews and rating
  • GBP calls, directions and website clicks
  • Local pack rank on four core keywords
  • Organic traffic and keywords
  • Authority Score and quality-audited referring domains
  • AI visibility and AI mentions
  • Consultation requests from forms and calls

Important Notes

Competitor review counts, local grid rankings and Core Web Vitals were not estimated and need live tools in week one. Targets are planning ranges, and any first-visit offer or secondary category must be confirmed as real for the practice.

Rankings, traffic and patient numbers cannot be guaranteed. Targets in these reports are planning goals, and the first month sets the baseline. Real return should be judged against your own data, such as the lifetime value of a new patient.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is a 5.0 rating with 126 reviews not enough on its own?

It proves trust, but not relevance for specific services and towns. The report identifies relevance as the biggest gap: services are not itemized on the profile, and reviews rarely mention the treatment or town.

Why split the services page into 17 pages?

Today all ten services share /services/, so no single page can target a term like Invisalign Fort Payne. The plan builds one hub, four parents and twelve children, each with one primary keyword and call to action.

Who is the main competitor for Skelton?

Carter Orthodontics is the one rival that overlaps the catchment, with offices in Rainsville and Scottsboro and town-level pages. The other specialists researched are more distance plays, and their review counts are pulled live in week one.

Will town pages be created for every nearby community?

Not automatically. Tier 1 towns come first, and Tier 2 pages wait until real proof exists, such as patients by ZIP, drive times and local reviews. The report warns that thin copy-paste town pages dilute the whole silo.

Does the report promise results?

No. The 90-day figures, such as 155 or more reviews and 250 or more visits, are planning ranges to manage toward. This is an independent portfolio study and does not claim any achieved outcome.
