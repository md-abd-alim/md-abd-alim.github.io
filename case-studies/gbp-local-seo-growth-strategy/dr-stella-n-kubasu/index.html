Dr. Stella N. Kubasu

GBP & Local SEO Growth Strategy · Upper Hill, Nairobi, Kenya

Business Overview

Dr. Stella N. Kubasu is a general and family dentist on Ralph Bunche Road in Upper Hill, Nairobi, Kenya, who has been in private practice since 1992. She holds a 4.9-star Google rating from 79 reviews, yet she has no website at all, so her Google Business Profile carries the whole digital load.

This independent portfolio study is a 27-page Google Business Profile and local SEO growth strategy dated October 2026. It builds from the public footprint and a consultant scorecard rather than third-party traffic exports, since there is no site to measure. It covers a technical blueprint for a new website, profile optimization, a Maps ranking and grid-tracking plan, seven service pages, tiered Nairobi area pages, reviews, Kenyan citations, AI search readiness, conversion paths and a 90-day roadmap.

This study covers a practice in Nairobi, Kenya, not the United States, and shows how I plan local growth for a practice that starts with no website.

These are independent portfolio studies based on publicly available information and third-party SEO estimates. The businesses were not contacted, are not clients and have not endorsed this work. No traffic, ranking or patient results are claimed.

Current Situation

The audit finds an elite reputation that is not yet converted into searchable, bookable web assets.

No website exists

There is no destination for organic search, no service or area pages, no schema, tracking or conversion funnel. The report scores website and technical SEO at 0 out of 10 and organic keyword visibility and content depth at 1, so every treatment search goes uncaptured.

Reputation is the strength

The 4.9-star rating from 79 reviews scores 9 out of 10, backed by a University of Nairobi BDS (1989) and practice since 1992. The weakness is that few reviews mention specific services, which limits relevance signals for treatment searches.

Profile is only partly built

The profile scores 5 out of 10. Services are only partly listed, the description is not keyword-aware or location-led, photos and video are thin, there is no regular posting or seeded Q&A, and there is no booking link to convert visitors.

Address is written two ways

The clinic appears as "Nelson Awori Building, 4th Floor, Wing A, Suite A2" and as "Prof. Nelson Awori Centre, Ralph Bunche Rd". Inconsistent name, address and phone data weakens Maps trust and gives AI tools conflicting facts.

Social and directory gaps

An Instagram account exists, but the Facebook link is a profile.php address and the LinkedIn slug does not match the doctor's name. There is no evidence of controlled listings on Kenyan healthcare directories, Bing Places or Apple Business Connect.

Crowded street, beatable gaps

Competitors include The Nairobi Dentist, Upper Hill Dental Centre, Molars and others, most with websites and blogs. None visibly owns a minimally invasive, tooth-saving family dentistry message, which is the positioning the report recommends.

What Happens If These Issues Are Not Fixed

If the website and profile gaps stay open, a strong reputation may keep reaching only patients who already know the clinic.

Invisible for treatment searches

With no pages, the practice cannot appear in organic results for root canal, fillings or family dentist searches, while nearby clinics that publish service pages capture that demand.

Profile traffic cannot convert

Patients who find the profile have no web destination, booking path or detailed service information, so interested searchers may call a competitor with a clearer booking route.

Inconsistent address data

Two address formats across channels can confuse patients, split citations and leave Google and AI assistants unsure which version is correct, weakening trust signals in Maps.

Review volume trails larger brands

At 79 reviews, the practice has weaker prominence than high-volume chains in the Maps pack. Without a steady review routine, the 4.9 rating alone may not close that gap.

Left out of AI answers

With no website, FAQ or entity content to quote, AI assistants have little to cite about the doctor or clinic, so recommendations may favor competitors with clear published answers.

Progress cannot be measured

Without GA4, Search Console and call or WhatsApp tracking, the clinic cannot tell which actions bring enquiries, so effort could drift toward tactics that do not help.

Recommendations & Action Plan

I recommend starting with fixes that need no website, then building the site, then adding authority, because each stage depends on the one before.

1. Lock the baseline and NAP

Capture a Week 1 rank-grid scan and set up GA4 and Search Console. Choose one canonical business name, address format and phone, and use it on the profile, website footer and every directory before any listings work begins.

2. Activate the Google Business Profile

Keep Dentist as primary and add only categories matching real services. List all 10 priority services, publish the roughly 640-character description led by experience and Upper Hill, add WhatsApp and booking links, 30-plus real photos, a short doctor video and seeded Q&A.

3. Build the website correctly from Day 1

Register a clear domain with HTTPS, a mobile-first design with a sticky call, WhatsApp and directions bar, fast loading, sitemap, canonical tags, clean URLs and Dentist, Person and FAQ schema. Target launch is Day 45, with privacy wording aligned to Kenya's Data Protection Act.

4. Publish seven service pages

Write family dentistry, root canal, tooth-colored fillings, pediatric, preventive, gum treatment and orthodontic pages. Each uses one blueprint: introduction, patient problem, treatment, benefits, process, aftercare, FAQs and a single call to action.

5. Add area pages in tiers

Launch Upper Hill, Nairobi CBD, Kilimani and Westlands pages first, with real directions, routes and local detail. Hold Lavington and Karen until patient stories and local proof exist, so no thin doorway pages are created.

6. Run a review and trust system

Send a service-specific WhatsApp request with a short link and a reception QR code, aiming for 8 to 10 new reviews monthly, replying within 48 hours and never offering incentives. Add a doctor bio showing KMPDB registration and credentials.

7. Clean citations and earn links

Audit and fix listings, then register Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Dabadoc, Vezeeta and the building listing in two batches. Pursue earned links from local GPs, schools, employers and alumni networks, targeting 8 to 10 by Day 90.

8. Prepare for AI search and report monthly

Add answer-first FAQs, entity facts and sameAs links, then test four named prompts monthly. Review a one-page scorecard each month and recalibrate targets at Day 30 using real GBP Insights and Search Console data.

Roadmap

Month 1: Foundation

Baseline scan, GA4 and Search Console, profile categories, services and description, NAP lock, review QR launch, and domain, design and content writing for the website.

Month 2: Growth

Website live by Day 45 with seven service pages and four Tier 1 area pages, schema, tracking and speed checks, citation batch 1, and weekly posts with the blog.

Month 3: Authority

Citation batch 2, local links and partnerships, AI optimization and entity profiles, then a Day 90 report and a six-month reforecast.

Expected Outcomes

These are planning goals from the report's KPI dashboard, to be recalibrated at Day 30, not promised results.

Review growth

Move from 79 reviews to 105 by Day 90, 130 by Month 6 and 190 by Month 12, holding a rating of 4.8 or higher.

A functioning website

Go from 0 to 20-plus indexed pages by Day 90, with organic sessions planned at 150 per month by Day 90 and 600 by Month 6.

Stronger Maps position

Aim for a top 5 position for "dentist Upper Hill" by Day 90 and top 3 by Month 6, measured on a grid scan.

More patient actions

Raise calls, WhatsApp chats and directions requests, and new-patient enquiries, against baselines captured in Week 1.

AI visibility

Be named in 1 of 4 tracked prompts by Day 90 and 2 of 4 by Month 6, measured through monthly tests.

More From the Report

E-E-A-T and medical trust

The report treats dental content as a high-stakes topic and maps experience, expertise, authority and trust. It recommends a years-in-practice timeline, consented patient stories, visible KMPDB registration, a doctor bio with Person schema, medically reviewed content, privacy information and treatment-risk disclaimers, since none exist online today.

Honest positioning

The strategy deliberately avoids specialist labels the doctor does not hold. It advises against the Endodontist and Orthodontist categories, covering root canals and simple braces through services instead, and builds the message around experienced, gentle, tooth-saving family dentistry for parents, Upper Hill professionals and anxious patients.

Conversion and Maps ranking

Every channel is designed to lead to one of five actions: call, WhatsApp, book, request a consultation or get directions. The plan adds reception rules, such as replying to WhatsApp within 15 minutes and logging patient source, and organizes Maps work around relevance, distance and prominence, with honest limits for Karen.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Dr. Kubasu need a website if her Google rating is 4.9?

The rating builds trust, but without a website there is nothing to rank for treatment searches, no booking path and nothing for AI assistants to cite. The plan turns the existing reputation into service pages, area pages and tracked enquiries.

Which Nairobi areas does the plan target first?

Tier 1 is Upper Hill, Nairobi CBD, Kilimani with Kileleshwa and Hurlingham, and Westlands with Parklands. Lavington and Karen come later, with honest drive-time guidance, because distance limits how far Maps visibility can reach.

Which services get dedicated pages?

Seven pages: family dentistry, root canal, tooth-colored fillings, pediatric, preventive, gum treatment and orthodontic. Each follows one patient-first blueprint with credentials, real photos, FAQs and one clear call to action.

How does the plan avoid overstating her qualifications?

It advises against Endodontist and Orthodontist categories and specialist labels she does not hold. Root canals and simple braces are covered through service pages, and the profile description cites her BDS, KMPDB registration and 30-plus years of practice.

Are these results the practice achieved?

No. This is an independent portfolio study of a publicly visible practice, not a client engagement. The targets are planning goals to be recalibrated at Day 30, and actual outcomes would depend on implementation and local competition.
