Google Business Profile & Local SEO Growth Strategy

Independent portfolio studies showing how I turn a local audit into a clear 90-day roadmap

What Is GBP & Local SEO Growth Strategy?

A GBP and local SEO growth strategy is a plan for improving how a dental practice appears in Google Maps and local search results. It starts with the Google Business Profile, then extends to reviews, citations, website pages, and the signals that tell Google your practice is relevant and trusted.

Google generally looks at three things for local results: relevance to the search, distance from the searcher, and prominence, meaning how well known and credible the business appears. You cannot change distance, but you can work on the other two in an organized way.

The strategy brings those parts together into one sequenced plan, usually over about 90 days. It builds on Google Business Profile optimization and connects to wider dental local SEO work.

Why It Matters for a Dental Practice

Maps is where many patients start

People searching for a dentist nearby often see map results first. Being visible there puts your practice in front of patients ready to choose.

The profile is your first impression

Photos, services, hours, and reviews often shape the decision before anyone visits your website. An incomplete profile can lose the patient early.

Three factors drive results

Relevance, distance, and prominence work together. Knowing which one is holding you back tells you where to focus effort.

Rankings vary by location

A practice may appear well near the office and poorly a few miles away. Grid tracking shows this pattern instead of relying on one search.

Trust signals matter

Genuine reviews, consistent business details, and clear provider information help patients and search engines judge reliability.

A roadmap prevents scattered effort

Without a sequence, practices tend to do random tasks. A 90-day plan puts the most useful actions first and assigns owners.

Step-by-Step: What a Dental SEO Consultant Checks

Here is the order I typically follow, from understanding where the practice stands to building a calendar of actions.

1. Audit the Google Business Profile

Check ownership, primary and secondary categories, name consistency, address, hours, phone, website link, services, attributes, photos, and any suspended or duplicate listings. Note what is missing or inaccurate.

2. Review relevance, distance, and prominence

Assess how well the profile and website match key searches, how the office location affects reach, and how the practice compares with nearby competitors on reviews and online presence.

3. Run Maps grid tracking

Check rankings for priority searches across a grid of points around the office. This shows where the practice appears strongly, where it fades, and which competitors dominate each area.

4. Evaluate the review profile

Look at review count, rating, recency, response habits, and the topics patients mention. Review how the office asks for feedback and confirm the process follows platform rules.

5. Check citations and consistency

Compare name, address, and phone details across major directories, health listings, and data sources. Fix conflicting or outdated entries and remove duplicates that could confuse search engines.

6. Review service and location pages

Confirm the website has useful pages for core treatments and each location, with consistent details, local context, and a link relationship to the Google profile.

7. Assess E-E-A-T signals

Look for evidence of experience and trust: dentist bios with credentials, real team photos, clear policies, accurate health information, and reputable mentions that show the practice is real and credible.

8. Build the 90-day roadmap

Turn findings into a dated plan covering profile fixes, review process, page improvements, citations, and reporting. Assign each task to an owner and set check-in dates.

What Happens If You Skip It

Local visibility problems often build slowly, and a practice may not notice until new patient calls decline.

Inaccurate or incomplete profiles

Wrong hours, missing services, or a poor category choice can reduce relevance and send patients away or to a competitor.

Reviews stall

If feedback is not requested consistently or responses are ignored, competitors with fresher, more active profiles can look more trustworthy.

Conflicting business details

Different names, addresses, or phone numbers across the web make it harder for search engines to trust which information is correct.

Blind spots in coverage

Without grid tracking, a practice may believe it ranks well based on one search, while whole neighborhoods rarely see it.

Website and profile do not match

If the profile promotes services the website barely mentions, Google has less evidence that the practice is relevant for those searches.

Rule violations

Keyword-stuffed names, fake locations, or improper review practices can lead to suspensions that are time-consuming to resolve.

My Approach

I focus on accurate, rule-compliant improvements that a practice can maintain, not shortcuts that risk the listing.

  1. Start with a clear baseline

    I document profile status, rankings, reviews, and calls first, so later changes can be judged against real starting points.

  2. Follow Google's guidelines

    Recommendations stay within platform rules, including naming, categories, and review practices, to protect the listing.

  3. Balance quick wins and long work

    Profile corrections come early, while reviews, pages, and local trust build over the full roadmap.

  4. Use the practice's real strengths

    Content and photos come from your team, services, and patients, so the profile reflects what you actually offer.

  5. Report in plain terms

    Updates focus on visibility, calls, and direction requests, with notes on what changed and what to do next.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does local SEO take to show results?

Profile corrections can be reflected within weeks, but reviews, pages, and local credibility usually build over several months. I use a 90-day roadmap as a first phase, then review the data to decide what to continue.

Can I just change my profile name to include keywords?

No. Google expects the profile name to match your real-world business name. Adding keywords or locations can lead to suspension. Relevance is better built through accurate categories, services, website content, and genuine reviews.

What is grid tracking and why does it matter?

Grid tracking checks your Maps ranking from many points around your office instead of one location. It shows where you are strong, where competitors dominate, and which neighborhoods may need more attention.

How many reviews do I need?

There is no magic number. Patients and search engines look at rating, recency, and whether feedback seems genuine. I focus on a steady, ethical process for asking, rather than chasing a specific count or offering incentives.

Does this work for practices with multiple locations?

Yes, but each location needs its own accurate profile, local page, and review process. I look at them individually and as a group, so branding stays consistent while each office gets proper local attention.
