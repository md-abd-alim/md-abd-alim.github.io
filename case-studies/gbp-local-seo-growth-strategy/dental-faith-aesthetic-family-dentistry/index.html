Dental Faith Aesthetic & Family Dentistry

GBP & Local SEO Growth Strategy · Nashville, TN

Business Overview

Dental Faith Aesthetic & Family Dentistry is a faith-based, bilingual (English and Spanish) family and cosmetic practice at 390 Harding Place in South Nashville, Tennessee, led by Dr. M. Paula Fedler. It already has a strong base: a 4.8-star Google profile with 271 reviews, 25 service pages and about 100 blog posts.

This 47-page independent portfolio study is a Google Business Profile and local SEO growth strategy for that publicly visible practice. It covers the market, an SEO baseline, a technical and profile audit, Maps ranking and grid tracking, competitors, keywords, AI search, service and location pages, trust, citations, conversion, and a 90-day roadmap with a KPI dashboard. Its central point is that the site attracts plenty of educational traffic but few patients searching to book, so the plan focuses on turning existing authority into local, bilingual, booking-intent visibility.

These are independent portfolio studies based on publicly available information and third-party SEO estimates. The businesses were not contacted, are not clients and have not endorsed this work. No traffic, ranking or patient results are claimed.

Current Situation

The audit finds a trusted, active practice whose search footprint leans toward reading rather than booking.

Informational traffic, few bookable searches

Of about 2.6K ranking keywords, 1.8K are informational and only 72 are transactional. Organic visits stand at 387 and are down 7.4 percent, so visitors mostly read and leave. One DIY-style post about removing a broken tooth at home ranks second.

Authority gap

The site has 882 referring domains and about 3.5K backlinks yet an Authority Score of just 11, which suggests low-quality or low-relevance links. The report calls for a link audit before building more, and for cautious cleanup of clearly spammy links.

A solid but incomplete profile

The owner-verified profile has hours, 18 services and recent posts, but no holiday hours, a non-https website link without tracking, no whitening or broken-tooth services, stock service images, and two posts that only reuse blog topics. The booking path and Q&A are not clearly set up.

Thin location and Spanish coverage

Only two location URLs exist for 11 target communities, and there are no Spanish-language location pages or visible language switch. The report treats bilingual care, with its large nearby Hispanic communities, as real demand and not a nice-to-have.

Review-heavy rivals and name ambiguity

Third-party listings show nearby competitors with far more reviews, such as Cane Ridge Dentist at about 1,436 and Nashville Family Dentistry at about 926. The term "faith dental" is also shared with unrelated practices in Arkansas and Alabama, so brand traffic can leak.

Technical and trust loose ends

The site is built on Elementor with many images and a review widget, and speed is assumed to need work. Image file names are screenshots, privacy pages are duplicated, a founding-year conflict (2009 versus 2013) exists, and one directory lists another business at the same suite.

What Happens If These Issues Are Not Fixed

If these gaps stay open, the practice's existing reputation will keep doing less work than it could.

Reading traffic never becomes patients

With so few transactional keywords, educational visits can keep rising or falling without producing calls, texts or appointments, because posts do not lead clearly to service pages and a booking action.

Review gap widens

Rivals with hundreds or over a thousand reviews hold a prominence advantage in Maps. Without a steady request routine, a 4.8 rating on 271 reviews becomes harder to defend as competitors keep adding more.

Bilingual demand goes to others

Without Spanish landing pages, GBP content and replies in the reviewer's language, searches such as dentista en Nashville may be answered by competitors that mention Spanish assistance.

AI tools cite pages but not the brand

The report notes pages are cited far more often than the practice is named. Weak entity signals and ambiguous branding risk AI answers that recommend other practices or confuse the name.

Conflicting data erodes trust

Mismatched founding years, the same-suite directory listing, inconsistent review counts and unsupported claims can lower trust for patients and weaken local signals.

Poor mobile experience

Heavy pages and no sticky call bar or online booking can lose mobile searchers, who often look for emergency or same-day care.

Recommendations & Action Plan

The recommendations run from quick profile fixes to page structure, trust and measurement, in this order.

1. Complete the Google Business Profile

Keep Dentist as primary and confirm secondary categories such as cosmetic dentist, emergency dental service, dental implants provider and teeth whitening. Add the missing whitening and broken-tooth services, use an https link with UTM tracking, add holiday hours, and publish the recommended 554-character description.

2. Build a photo, post and Q&A routine

Follow the photo plan for exterior, team, equipment and consented patient photos, and aim for two varied posts a week with offers, services, FAQs and doctor content. Answer questions on same-day emergencies, Spanish, insurance, no-insurance options and parking.

3. Start a compliant review system

Aim for 8 to 12 new reviews a month through in-person asks, same-day text links, a Spanish version and a QR code. Reply within 48 hours in the reviewer's language. No incentives, no filtering of unhappy patients, and no confirming patient status.

4. Track Maps visibility with a ranking grid

Run a 7x7 grid around 37211 each month for dentist near me, family dentist, emergency dentist, implants, cosmetic dentist and dentista en Nashville, with separate grids for Antioch and Brentwood. This shows where the practice appears and where rivals are stronger.

5. Upgrade service pages and structure

Strengthen seven priority pages (implants, emergency, cosmetic, crowns, veneers, root canals, bridges) with a standard blueprint: FAQs, costs, credentials, calls to action and Service and FAQ schema. Add implant-supported dentures, children's, new-patient and smile makeover pages.

6. Launch location and Spanish pages

Create Tier 1 pages for Antioch, South Nashville and Brentwood, then Franklin, Hermitage and La Vergne with Smyrna. Each needs unique local content, not template swaps. Build a Spanish hub with emergency, implants, crowns and general care pages and a language toggle.

7. Strengthen trust, citations and links

Publish one consistent practice timeline, credential details and reviewer credits. Fix the conflicting directory listing, claim healthcare and local profiles, and pursue community, chamber, media and association links while auditing the 882 existing referring domains.

8. Improve conversion and set up tracking

Add a sticky mobile bar with call, text and book options, a shorter form, online booking or callback request, and trust elements near calls to action. Track calls, texts, form fills and direction clicks in GA4 and GBP Insights.

Roadmap

Month 1: Foundation

Update categories, services, link and hours, set up tracking, run the grid baseline and citation audit, launch the review system, make technical fixes, add photos, and audit existing posts.

Month 2: Growth

Upgrade the implants, emergency and cosmetic pages, publish the Antioch, South Nashville and Brentwood pages, launch Spanish pages, add booking or callback, publish the first articles, and review the first grid.

Month 3: Authority

Complete directory listings, run local link outreach, add FAQ blocks and entity schema, publish Tier 2 area pages and more articles, then produce a 90-day report for the next quarter.

Expected Outcomes

The report frames these as 90-day planning targets, to be replaced with real baselines once data is exported.

More GBP actions

A planning target of 15 to 25 percent more calls, website clicks and direction requests than the Month 0 baseline.

More booking-intent keywords

A target of 30 to 50 percent more transactional keywords, alongside 15 to 25 percent more organic traffic.

Faster review growth

Adding 25 to 35 reviews while keeping the rating at 4.8 or higher.

Better local grid visibility

Improved average rank in the monthly grid for core keywords near 37211, with entry into the local top 10 for Antioch and Brentwood.

Clearer AI visibility

AI mentions and visibility score above the current 4 and 14.

More From the Report

E-E-A-T and medical trust

The report scores experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness separately. Suggested fixes include one consistent practice timeline, license and continuing education details, medical-review bylines on posts, healthcare directory profiles, and substantiating or softening unsupported claims. It also advises adding medical disclaimers and an emergency booking call to action on DIY-style posts.

AI search optimization

Because AI tools cite pages but rarely name the practice, the plan uses one consistent name, Dentist, Person, Service and FAQ schema with sameAs links, answer-first FAQ blocks, and comparison tables. Sample target queries include a Spanish-speaking family dentist near Berry Hill and what to do for a cracked tooth in Nashville.

Competitor set and keyword overlap

The report separates real local-pack competitors from keyword-overlap domains. Faith-named practices in Arkansas and a Chile site are excluded from local benchmarking, while Spanish-language brands are reviewed only for content ideas. Review counts come from third-party listings and are to be confirmed in the first grid audit.

What I Would Measure

  • GBP calls, website clicks and direction requests
  • Google review count and rating
  • Organic traffic
  • Transactional and commercial keyword counts
  • Local grid average rank
  • AI visibility, mentions and cited pages

Important Notes

The 90-day figures are planning targets, not guarantees of rankings, traffic or revenue. Several profile and technical items, such as secondary categories, schema depth and page speed, were assumed and need confirming, as do competitor review counts and doctor credentials before publication.

Rankings, traffic and patient numbers cannot be guaranteed. Targets in these reports are planning goals, and the first month sets the baseline. Real return should be judged against your own data, such as the lifetime value of a new patient.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main finding of the Dental Faith report?

The practice has strong trust and content, with 4.8 stars on 271 reviews and about 100 posts, but only 2.7 percent of its ranking keywords are transactional. The plan aims to convert educational visibility into local, bilingual, booking-oriented searches.

Why does the report emphasize Spanish-language pages?

The practice is bilingual and sits near South Nashville and Antioch, which have large Hispanic communities. The report found no Spanish location pages or visible language switch, so it recommends a Spanish hub, GBP content and replies in the reviewer's language.

How does it handle other practices using the name Faith Dental?

It excludes Arkansas practices and a Chilean site from local ranking benchmarks, monitors the brand search results, and recommends consistent naming, entity schema and wording such as Dental Faith Nashville to reduce confusion for people and AI tools.

How does the review plan stay within Google's rules?

It plans honest requests at checkout, by text and by QR code, with a Spanish option and a single follow-up. It forbids incentives and filtering unhappy patients, and warns against confirming patient status in public replies.

Are the 90-day targets promised results?

No. They are planning targets based on typical local SEO ramp-up and are meant to be replaced with real baselines from GBP Insights and Search Console. The report states they are estimates and not guarantees.
