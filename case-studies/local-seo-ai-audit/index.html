Local SEO & AI Audit

Short, scored audits that show what to fix first in 30 days

What Is Local SEO & AI Audit?

A Local SEO and AI audit is a short, scored review of how well a dental practice shows up and is understood online. It covers the technical health of the website, the accuracy and consistency of the Google Business Profile and local listings, how ready the practice is for AI-powered search, and the trust signals patients see.

Instead of a long technical dump, the audit gives each area a score and explains what the score means in plain language. Findings are ranked by importance, so you can tell what is urgent, what is worthwhile, and what can wait.

It ends with a practical 30-day plan. This makes it a good first step before larger work, and it often pairs with a deeper dental SEO audit when more detail is needed.

Why It Matters for a Dental Practice

It shows where you stand

A scored snapshot replaces guesswork, so you know which areas are healthy and which need attention.

Local consistency affects visibility

Mismatched names, addresses, phone numbers, or hours can confuse patients and weaken trust in your listings.

AI search is changing discovery

Patients increasingly get answers from AI tools. Clear, accurate, well-structured information helps your practice be understood and cited.

Technical issues quietly hold sites back

Slow pages, broken links, or indexing problems can undermine good content without anyone noticing.

Trust drives booking

Reviews, provider details, and clear contact information influence whether a visitor calls or leaves.

A short plan gets done

Thirty days of prioritized tasks is manageable, and progress builds momentum for larger projects.

Step-by-Step: What a Dental SEO Consultant Checks

Here is how I move through a short audit, from the foundation to the actions that follow.

1. Check technical basics

Review indexing, site speed, mobile usability, security, broken pages, redirects, and sitemap setup. Note issues that block patients or search engines from reaching key pages.

2. Review on-page fundamentals

Look at titles, headings, meta descriptions, and treatment page structure. Confirm each important service has a clear, findable page.

3. Audit Google Business Profile

Check categories, services, description, hours, photos, attributes, and links. Confirm the profile is claimed, verified, and matches how the practice actually operates.

4. Test local consistency

Compare name, address, and phone details across the website, profile, and major directories. Flag duplicates, old addresses, and conflicting information.

5. Assess AI search readiness

Check whether content answers patient questions clearly, whether structured data is present and valid, and whether the practice is described consistently across the web.

6. Evaluate trust signals

Review reviews and responses, provider bios and credentials, real photos, privacy and accessibility basics, and clear ways to contact or book.

7. Score each area

Assign a simple score to technical, local, AI readiness, and trust, with short notes explaining the reasoning so the numbers are not a black box.

8. Build the 30-day plan

Sort findings into week-by-week actions by impact and effort, naming who should handle each task, such as the dentist, office manager, or web developer.

What Happens If You Skip It

Small gaps in these areas often go unnoticed for years because nothing obviously breaks.

Unnoticed technical problems

Pages may be blocked from indexing or load slowly on phones, which can cost visibility and patient patience.

Inconsistent business details

An old phone number or address on a directory can send patients to the wrong place and weaken confidence in your listings.

Incomplete profiles

A Google profile with missing categories or services gives patients and Google less to work with.

Invisible to AI answers

Vague or unstructured content may not be used when AI tools summarize options for patients.

Weak trust signals

Missing provider information or unanswered reviews can make a good practice look uncertain.

No clear next step

Without prioritization, owners either do nothing or try everything at once, and little improves.

My Approach

I keep the audit short, readable, and focused on what to do next.

  1. Score, then explain

    Every score comes with a plain-language reason, so you understand the finding, not just the number.

  2. Cover local and AI together

    I review traditional local factors and AI readiness in one pass, since they overlap more than most people expect.

  3. Prioritize ruthlessly

    I rank issues by impact and effort so the first month focuses on the most useful work.

  4. Assign owners

    Each task names who can complete it, which makes the plan easier to act on.

  5. Stay honest about limits

    I note what I could not verify and avoid overstating what any fix will deliver.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is this different from a full SEO audit?

It is shorter and more focused. You get a scored overview of technical health, local consistency, AI readiness, and trust, plus a 30-day plan. A full audit goes deeper into crawl data, content, and links when that level of detail is needed.

What does AI search readiness mean?

It means your information is clear, accurate, and consistent enough for AI tools to understand and repeat correctly. That includes plain answers to patient questions, structured data, and matching details across your website and listings. No one can guarantee being cited.

Will I understand the scores?

Yes. Each score has a short explanation of what was checked, what was found, and why it matters. The goal is a document you can share with your office manager or web developer and act on without translation.

Who does the 30-day plan work fall on?

That depends on the task. Some items suit the dentist or office manager, such as profile updates or review replies, while others need a developer. The plan labels who is best placed to handle each item.

Do you need access to my accounts?

Usually view or limited access to Google Business Profile, Search Console, and Analytics helps the audit be more accurate. I only request what is needed, and you can grant read-only access and remove it whenever you like.
