West Coast Physical Therapy

Local SEO & AI Audit · Bakersfield, CA

Business Overview

West Coast Physical Therapy Lab is a physical therapy practice at 5630 District Blvd #105 in Bakersfield, California. This is a non-dental business, so the study shows the same audit method working outside dentistry. Its site is built on Wix and carries expert articles by licensed physical therapist Dr. Anthony Harrell, including pelvic floor topics, plus an online booking link through Jane App.

This independent portfolio study is a 7-page digital presence and AI search audit dated July 30, 2026. It scores the website, Google Business Profile, local SEO, social media, AI search and conversion out of 10, ranks the top 10 issues and ends with a 30-day quick-win list. Good content sits on a thin technical and local foundation, so the opportunity score is high. Unverified items are flagged rather than guessed. The plan is framed as recommendations, not guaranteed outcomes.

This audit covers a non-dental business (physical therapy) and shows the same research method applied outside dentistry.

These are independent portfolio studies based on publicly available information and third-party SEO estimates. The businesses were not contacted, are not clients and have not endorsed this work. No traffic, ranking or patient results are claimed.

Current Situation

The audit finds credible expert content and working booking, held back by missing metadata, an under-built profile and almost no local or AI signals.

Good content, thin optimization

Content quality scores 7 of 10, blog strategy 7 and E-E-A-T 8, thanks to articles by Dr. Harrell, a licensed DPT. But title tags, meta descriptions, on-page SEO and primary keyword optimization all score 3, and "Physical Therapy Bakersfield" is absent from the main headings.

Heavy Wix pages

Page speed scores 4 and Core Web Vitals 3 because the homepage uses many large images and scripts. Technical SEO scores 4, image optimization 4, internal linking 3 and schema markup 2, with no LocalBusiness or FAQ structured data seen.

Google Business Profile gaps

The profile has a working booking link (8), but secondary categories score 3, services 3, photos 4, and videos, weekly posts and Q&A all score 0. Rating, review count and review responses could not be verified from public data.

Few local signals

NAP consistency scores 9, but citations score 3, local backlinks 1 and location pages 3. Only the Bakersfield office page exists, and Maps ranking was judged likely outside the top three for target terms, an assessment rather than a measured grid.

Minimal social and AI presence

Facebook scores 3, Instagram 2 and YouTube 0, though Dr. Harrell has 264 LinkedIn followers. AI visibility is near zero: Google AI Overviews 0, ChatGPT 2, Gemini 1, Perplexity 0, and AI readiness 1 because structured data and AI-ready content are missing.

Booking works, trust proof is absent

Online booking, click to call and the contact form each score 8. Live chat, trust badges, testimonials and a before-and-after or case-study section all score 0, so a visitor sees little outside validation before booking.

What Happens If These Issues Are Not Fixed

If these gaps remain, a clinic with real clinical expertise stays hard to find and hard to trust online.

Poor search snippets

Without custom titles and meta descriptions, search engines choose the text shown for the clinic, which can bury Bakersfield and the services patients are searching for.

Lost visitors to slow pages

Large images and a heavy template likely slow loading, particularly on mobile, so prospective patients may leave before reading the articles or reaching the booking link.

Invisible on Maps

An underfilled profile with few categories, photos, posts or Q&A gives Google little relevance data, so competing clinics with fuller listings may appear first for nearby searches.

Thin credibility

With no visible testimonials, badges, insurance logos or confirmed reviews, patients choosing among clinics have little external proof, even though the clinician is licensed and expert.

Expertise not reaching patients

Strong articles that are barely interlinked, lack schema and are not repurposed on social channels or video may never reach the people searching for pelvic floor or rehabilitation help.

Left out of AI answers

With no structured data or AI-ready content, assistants and AI Overviews have little reason to cite the clinic, so patients using those tools may see other providers instead.

Recommendations & Action Plan

I recommend fixing basic visibility first, then building the local and trust signals the site lacks.

1. Add titles and descriptions

Write custom meta titles and descriptions for the homepage, service pages and blog posts, using local terms such as Bakersfield physical therapy. This is the quickest fix for how the clinic appears in results and supports every later keyword step.

2. Compress images and add alt text

Resize and compress large images, and add descriptive alt text. This directly addresses the page speed and Core Web Vitals scores and helps accessibility, without changing the design or content.

3. Complete the Google Business Profile

Add a full description, accurate primary and secondary categories, itemized services, interior and exterior photos, and a few posts such as new patient information. Confirm the existing categories and rating in the dashboard first, since public data was limited.

4. Build an honest review habit

Ask satisfied patients for Google reviews and respond promptly to every review, new and existing. Reviews and replies support local ranking and give prospective patients the proof the site currently lacks.

5. Claim consistent citations

Create or claim listings on Yelp, Healthgrades and the local chamber with the same name, address and phone already used on the site. Consistent directory listings also begin to build local links, currently scored 1.

6. Implement schema

Use Wix SEO tools to add LocalBusiness and Service structured data for treatments. Schema is the foundation for rich results and for AI systems recognizing what the clinic offers, and it addresses the 2 out of 10 schema score.

7. Add trust proof

Add relevant certifications such as APTA, insurance logos and member badges to the footer, and collect a few patient testimonials with consent for the homepage. These cost little and answer the zero-scored trust and testimonial areas.

8. Activate social and click to call

Post short introductory videos and photos about the clinic and therapists on Facebook and Instagram, fix the Instagram links that lead to unrelated accounts, and make sure the phone number is clickable on mobile.

Roadmap

Days 1-10: Basics

Add meta titles and descriptions, compress images with alt text, and confirm the profile's categories and rating in the dashboard.

Days 11-20: Profile and proof

Complete the profile with description, services, photos and first posts, start asking for reviews, add trust badges and collect initial testimonials.

Days 21-30: Local and structure

Claim directory listings with consistent details, add LocalBusiness and Service schema, and start posting photos and short videos on social channels.

More From the Report

Why this audit matters outside dentistry

Physical therapy is also a local, trust-based health service, so the same six-area framework applies: website, profile, local signals, social, AI search and conversion. The report adapts the language, for example APTA badges and pelvic floor content, while keeping the scoring method identical to the dental audits.

E-E-A-T and expert content

E-E-A-T scores 8 of 10 because the articles are written by a licensed DPT, Dr. Anthony Harrell, on specialized topics such as pelvic floor health. The recommendation is to make that expertise visible with interlinking, author information, schema, testimonials and badges so both patients and AI systems can verify it.

How unverified items are handled

Several items, including Google rating, review count, categories and the profile description, were not publicly visible. The report scores them cautiously and flags them as likely or unverified rather than inventing figures. Anyone acting on the profile section should confirm those details with dashboard access first.

What I Would Measure

  • Google reviews, rating and response rate
  • Google Business Profile posts, photos and services listed
  • Page speed and Core Web Vitals
  • Local directory citations and local backlinks
  • Online bookings, calls and contact form submissions
  • AI search mentions and citations

Important Notes

The report relies on public pages and says plainly that many profile, review and speed items could not be verified, so some scores are likely-based assessments. Scores are the audit's own judgment, and Maps ranking was estimated rather than measured with a rank grid.

Rankings, traffic and patient numbers cannot be guaranteed. Targets in these reports are planning goals, and the first month sets the baseline. Real return should be judged against your own data, such as the lifetime value of a new patient.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why audit a physical therapy clinic in a dental portfolio?

It shows that the same local SEO and AI search audit method works for any local health service. The six areas and scoring stay the same, while recommendations such as APTA badges and pelvic floor content are tailored to physical therapy.

What is the clinic's biggest strength?

Its expert content. Articles by licensed physical therapist Dr. Anthony Harrell earn 7 for content quality and 8 for E-E-A-T, and online booking, click to call and the contact form each score 8, so the foundation for conversion exists.

Why is the AI search score only 1 out of 10?

The site has no structured data or AI-tailored content, and nothing indicated visibility in Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity. Schema, FAQs and clearer local content are the starting points I recommend.

Are the review figures confirmed?

No. The Google rating, review count and review responses could not be verified from public data, so they were scored 0 as unverified. The first step is checking them in the profile dashboard and setting a baseline.

Is this a client engagement with proven results?

No. This is an independent portfolio study of a publicly visible clinic. The 30-day plan contains recommendations, and any outcome would depend on implementation, competition and the clinic's own data.
